# WholeStory: the combined product

WholeStory helps people turn confusing, recurring symptoms into a clear story clinicians can understand, so they can be heard sooner. It is an advocate for communicating an experience, never an AI doctor.

## The merged weekend workflow

1. Start without an account. Speak, type, or use optional structured fields.
2. Capture explicit cycle dates/day, location and numeric pain score, bleeding, fatigue, pain with sex/bowel movements/urination, gastrointestinal symptoms, interventions and reported effects, and impact on everyday life. Unknown stays unknown.
3. Review source-quoted AI extraction or form-generated statements. Hear one optional question, answer by voice or text, or skip.
4. Explore dated entries, documented cycles, and numeric scores without filling missing days or assuming cycle length.
5. Open a recurring observation to see its supporting entries. Multiple recorded cycles describe recurrence, not a cause, probability, or future prediction.
6. Review a clinician brief, select suggested or personal questions, and export a concise PDF with an optional evidence appendix.

## Three fictional personas

- Maya combines severe cyclical pain, bowel-related pain, fatigue, and missed classes.
- Alex demonstrates persistent pain outside period days, gastrointestinal/urinary symptoms, and cancelled activities.
- Jules combines mild cramps with little disruption and patient-reported relief after rest/interventions, without making an effectiveness claim.

Each uses hand-authored synthetic entries over 56 days with two explicitly recorded fictional cycles, gaps, and (where applicable) explicit symptom-free reports. Switching personas never mixes their counts or personal journal records. Historical demo entries from the first build remain stored separately.

## Clinical and data boundaries

“These patterns may be worth discussing with a healthcare professional. This tool does not diagnose endometriosis or replace medical care.”

- WHO and ESHRE inform general symptom categories and communication context. NHS pelvic-pain guidance informs the always-available urgent-care section. NICE and ACOG remain linked as additional resources; automated access to their full pages was limited in the first build.
- EHP-30 is a copyrighted, named quality-of-life instrument. This app does not reproduce its questionnaire, administer it, or claim an EHP-30 score. Our daily-impact questions are original, unvalidated documentation prompts.
- KI EndoLIST is restricted and describes 34 diagnosed participants. We did not access patient files, train on it, or use it for diagnostic validation.
- Synthea was reviewed as an optional synthetic-data resource. Current personas are original fictional records, not Synthea output.
- AI extracts structured facts only after consent; report statistics, recurring observations, and suggested appointment questions are deterministic. No diagnosis, risk score, causal treatment claim, or personalized treatment advice.
- Urgent-care guidance is general and always available. It is not an automated triage assessment; absence of a prompt is not reassurance.
